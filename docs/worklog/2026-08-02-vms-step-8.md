## Criterion

> *Scheduled health sweep + «آخرین اتصال» per camera. Done when a dead camera is visible as dead,
> not as a black square.*

## The trap in this step

**A health probe is itself a puller**, and step 7 established a site has room for exactly one. A
naive sweep would compete with the person watching — and the symptom would be the picture breaking
up every five minutes, blamed on the camera.

Two rules, both verified live:

1. **A camera somebody is watching is never probed.** go2rtc already holds a live connection to it,
   which is better evidence than any probe. The sweep reads go2rtc's `consumers` count and skips
   those. Measured with a viewer attached: `1 camera(s): 1 up, 0 down, 1 skipped as already watched`.
   With nobody watching: `0 skipped`.
2. **The probe is `DESCRIBE`, never `PLAY`.** It asks "is the stream there" for about a kilobyte.
   Pulling video to check whether video can be pulled would spend the very thing being measured.

## Where it runs, and why not here

On the **VPS**, on a timer, reporting back to `POST /api/VmsGateway/health` with the same shared
token as the config route. That machine already holds the credentials and already talks to the
cameras; probing a hundred devices from the production box is exactly the traffic this design keeps
off it.

The sweep builds its target list from **the streams block the API just served** — the same text
go2rtc is running — so a probe cannot drift from what is actually being served. `-main` entries are
skipped: same device, and probing both would be two probes for one camera.

## Only success moves the clock

`LastSeenUtc` answers *"when did this camera last work"*. A failed sweep leaves it alone, so the UI
shows the gap growing. Writing "now" on a failure would erase the one fact an operator needs.

Proven with one real camera and one that does not exist (`192.0.2.99`):

```
vms-health: 2 camera(s): 1 up, 1 down, 0 skipped
{"reported":2,"recorded":1,"unknown":0}

StreamKey   Host           LastSeen
baneh-01    78.39.233.70   2026-08-02T18:00:08+00:00
baneh-02    192.0.2.99     (never)
```

The dead one stays `(never)` rather than being stamped with a false "now". Whole sweep: **7.5 s**,
most of it the timeout on the unreachable host.

`null` is also not "offline" — it means never checked. A camera added five minutes ago has not been
swept yet, and calling that «بی‌ارتباط» would accuse a working camera of being broken, which is the
exact failure this feature exists to prevent. The UI shows it grey, not red.

## A dead camera now looks dead

The criterion's wording is the point: a black rectangle is also what a night-time courtyard looks
like, so the person who has to go and fix it cannot tell the difference. A tile whose player errors
now shows an overlay — «بی‌ارتباط» plus «آخرین اتصال: ۲ ساعت پیش», which is the *sweep's* answer
rather than the tile's, because a tile that just failed says nothing about whether the camera has
been gone for a minute or since Tuesday.

`lib/lastSeen.ts` is shared by the wall and the admin table so the two can never disagree about what
"recently" means.

## Why the timer is installed but not enabled

`vms-health.timer` points at `https://api.myceo.ir/api/VmsGateway`, which **does not exist yet**.
Enabling it now would fire a guaranteed failure every five minutes and fill the journal with noise
that means nothing. **Step 9 enables it**, after the API is deployed.

Five minutes is the interval, with a 30-second randomised delay. Slow on purpose: it is how long an
operator waits before «آخرین اتصال» tells them something is wrong, and at a hundred cameras it is
about twenty outbound connections a minute. Ten seconds would be six hundred — a scan.
